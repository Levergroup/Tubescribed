The **best AI tool to repurpose YouTube videos** depends on what you want the video to become. If you want short vertical clips, use a clipping tool such as OpusClip. If you want the same video posted to many channels automatically, use Repurpose.io. If you want the spoken words turned into a blog post, newsletter, SOP or social posts, use a transcript-first tool such as TubeScribed or Castmagic. Below are five honest picks, today's pricing from each tool's own site, and a real 9-minute video we ran through TubeScribed, including what we had to fix.

One rule before the list: no tool is the best at everything. That includes ours.

## Why there is no single best AI tool to repurpose YouTube videos

"Repurposing" covers three very different jobs:

- **Video to video.** Cut a long video into short clips, reframe them for mobile and add captions.
- **Video to distribution.** Take a video you already published and post it to other platforms without re-uploading by hand.
- **Video to text.** Turn what was said into written content: articles, emails, posts, show notes, training docs.

Whether you search for an AI tool to repurpose video content or a YouTube content repurposing tool, you'll find all three jobs in the results. Most "best repurposing tools" lists mix them together, so a clipping tool gets compared with a writing tool. That's like comparing a camera with a word processor. Pick the job first, then the tool.

### 1. OpusClip: best for short clips

OpusClip turns longer videos into short clips. Its [pricing page](https://www.opus.pro/pricing) lists auto reframe and AI captions, and shows YouTube among its import sources. When we checked it today, the plans were:

- **Free:** $0 with 60 credits per month. Clips have a watermark, and the page says clips stop being exportable after 3 days.
- **Starter:** $15/month, monthly billing only, 150 credits per month.
- **Pro:** $29/month, or $14.50/month billed annually ($174/year).

**Good for:** podcasters and creators who want vertical clips from long episodes.

**Not good for:** blog posts, newsletters or anything written.

### 2. Repurpose.io: best for automatic cross-posting

Repurpose.io's [pricing page](https://repurpose.io/pricing) sums it up as "Post once. Your content gets repurposed everywhere else, hands-free." It works with video and audio and connects to platforms such as YouTube Shorts, Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and X. Plans start at $35/month (Starter), then $79/month (Pro) and $179/month (Agency), with about 17% off yearly. The free trial lets you publish 10 videos with no credit card.

**Good for:** creators who already make clips and want them posted everywhere on a schedule.

**Not good for:** creating new written content. The pricing page describes publishing video and audio, not writing articles or emails.

### 3. Descript: best for editing what you already recorded

Descript's [pricing page](https://www.descript.com/pricing) describes text-based editing: you edit video and audio by editing the words. The free plan includes 60 media minutes per month and 100 one-time AI credits. Paid plans per person are Hobbyist ($16 annual or $24 monthly, 10 media hours), Creator ($24 annual or $35 monthly, 30 media hours) and Business ($50 annual or $65 monthly, 40 media hours).

**Good for:** teams that record and edit their own videos and podcasts.

**Not good for:** quickly turning someone's published YouTube video into a written asset. It's an editor first.

### 4. Castmagic: best for many text assets from audio and video

Castmagic's [pricing page](https://www.castmagic.io/pricing) says "Create 100 Content Assets from 1 Media File" and names summaries, long-form articles, email newsletters, LinkedIn posts and show notes among them. Its integrations table lists YouTube import on its Hobby, Starter and Team plans. Billed annually, Hobby is $19/month ($239/year) with 30 transcribed hours, Starter is $48/month with 100 hours and Team is $139/month with 400 hours and 5 seats.

**Good for:** podcast and media teams who want lots of text assets from recordings.

**Not good for:** anyone who wants to pay per video instead of a library of hours. Compare it with your volume.

### 5. TubeScribed: best for turning YouTube videos into business documents

TubeScribed takes a public YouTube URL, transcribes the **audio** (so it works with or without captions), then turns the transcript into one of 15 output types: Blog Post, Email Newsletter, Social Posts, Brand SOP, Content Ideas, X Thread, Show Notes, FAQ Document, Key Takeaways, Slide Outline, Sales Script, Training Guide, Case Study, Press Release and Ad Copy.

Brand workspaces save your tone, terminology and style so outputs match your brand voice (3 on Pro, unlimited on Agency). Pricing is per credit: 1 credit covers up to 10 minutes of video, rounded up, or 1 AI output. New users get free credits with no credit card. Pro is $19/month for 30 credits and Agency is $49/month for 100 credits and 5 team members. Credit packs never expire.

**Good for:** marketers, agencies, coaches and operators who want a YouTube video to become something they can publish or hand to a team.

**Not good for:** short video clips, local files, or videos longer than 90 minutes.

## The DIY option: an AI chat assistant and a pasted transcript

You don't need a dedicated tool to start. Open the video on YouTube, use the **Show transcript** panel described in [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15930243?hl=en), copy the text and paste it into the AI chat assistant you already use. Ask for a blog post or three social posts.

This is exactly what the video in our example below demonstrates. It's flexible and cheap if you already pay for an assistant. The trade-offs: it only works when the video has captions, long transcripts are tedious to copy, and you rebuild your prompt and brand voice every time.

## Real example: we ran a 9-minute video through TubeScribed

We picked a public tutorial that is itself about this topic: [How to Repurpose Your Content with AI (Double Your Traffic)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6ezPWET1ap4), from an "AI for beginners" YouTube channel whose host introduces himself as Ryan. It runs 9 minutes 19 seconds. He pastes a YouTube transcript into an AI chat assistant to get a blog post, social posts and a newsletter, then shows a custom GPT for better blog posts, and mentions OpusClip for short clips from his podcast.

We pasted the link into TubeScribed. Audio recognition didn't succeed on this video, so TubeScribed fell back to the captions and labelled the transcript "from captions" (1,815 words). That used 1 credit. We then asked for **Social Posts**, which used 1 more credit, so 2 in total. The result was five posts for LinkedIn, Instagram and Facebook, 816 words in all.

Here is a short excerpt from the second post, a how-to list:

> 1. **The Extraction:** Don't just ask AI to "summarize." Feed the raw transcript into a custom GPT or Claude and ask for an H2/H3 structured blog post focused on a specific SEO keyword.
> 2. **The Micro-Content:** Take that structured blog and prompt the AI to identify the 3 most controversial or counter-intuitive points. Turn these into standalone social posts.
> 3. **The Visual Layer:** Use tools like Opus Clip to scan the video for high-engagement moments.

Steps 1 and 3 match the video closely. Step 2 is the tool's own idea, not something the speaker said. It's a reasonable idea, but you should know where it came from.

**What we had to fix:**

- **Invented numbers.** Other posts said people spend "10 hours filming", that AI turns "a 40-hour work week" into "a 40-second prompt", and asked if you are "leaving 90% of your views on the cutting room floor". None of that is in the video. The real numbers he shares are modest: some clips got 400 to 500 views and one got almost 2,000. We would delete the invented figures or use his.
- **Overclaiming.** One post promised a full campaign "while you drink your morning coffee". The speaker says the opposite twice: it's "not a silver bullet" and you would "have to come in and polish". We would keep his honesty.
- **A stale year.** One post said "In 2024". Remove dates the video doesn't give.

Our verdict: the structure and the core workflow were accurate and quick to edit. The hooks needed fact-checking, which is normal for any AI draft. Budget a few minutes per post to check every number against the video.

## How to choose the best AI tool to repurpose YouTube videos

Ask one question: what should the video become?

1. **Short videos.** Use OpusClip or another clipping tool.
2. **The same videos, posted everywhere.** Use Repurpose.io.
3. **A cleaner edit of your own recording.** Use Descript.
4. **Lots of text assets from a podcast or media library.** Compare Castmagic's hour-based plans with your volume.
5. **Blog posts, newsletters, SOPs or social posts from public YouTube videos.** Use TubeScribed, then edit.
6. **One video a month.** A pasted transcript and the assistant you already use may be enough.

Many teams end up with two tools: one for clips and one for text. That's fine. They do different jobs.

Whatever you choose, keep a human in the loop. Google's guidance on [creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) asks whether content provides "original information, reporting, research, or analysis", and it warns against using generative AI to produce lots of text "without manual oversight or curation". Repurposing works best when you add your own examples and check every claim.

## When TubeScribed is not the best AI tool to repurpose YouTube videos

TubeScribed is the wrong pick if:

- **You want short video clips.** It produces written content, not video. Use a clipping tool.
- **You want automatic posting.** It doesn't publish to social platforms for you. You download outputs as .txt and post them yourself or with your scheduler.
- **Your files aren't on YouTube.** It works with public YouTube videos only, not private videos or local files.
- **Your videos run longer than 90 minutes.** That's the current limit.

If you want written business content from YouTube videos, it's a strong fit. TubeScribed also works inside Claude as a connector and through a developer API, if you want to build it into your own workflow: see [the Claude integration](/integrations/claude).

## A simple repurposing workflow you can start this week

1. Pick one public YouTube video you're allowed to reference, ideally your own.
2. Get the transcript, then skim it for the two or three ideas worth keeping.
3. Generate one format for one channel. Our guide to [repurposing a YouTube video for social media](/blog/repurpose-youtube-video-to-social-media) covers the social side, and the [YouTube transcript to blog post guide](/blog/youtube-transcript-to-blog-post) covers articles.
4. Fact-check first: every number and quote must be in the video.
5. Next week, reuse the same transcript for a second format, such as a [video-to-newsletter email](/blog/video-to-newsletter-ai).
